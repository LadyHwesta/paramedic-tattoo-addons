Reference

Who does what

Today it's all you. This is here for the day it isn't.

RoleOwns
PractitionerPatient records, conditions, consent, patch tests, treatment sessions, and scheduling — everything, while it's just you.
Front Desk (if you bring someone on)Confirming or rescheduling appointment requests, marking no-shows — without needing access to clinical records.
PatientTheir own page — requesting an appointment, seeing their upcoming and past visits, cancelling with enough notice.

Part 1

One-time setup

Do this once, in order, before your first real patient.

Add yourself as a practitioner

  1. Open Paramedical Tattoo › Configuration › Practitioners › New.

  2. Set User to your own login.

  3. Click into Working Hours, type a name like “Studio Hours”, and choose Create and Edit to set the days and times you're open. This is what the booking page uses to know when you're free — time off you add there is respected automatically.

  4. Add your body-art/cosmetology licence number while you're there. The NPI field can stay blank — it's only for if you ever bill insurance.

One thing to know

Creating or editing a Working Hours calendar needs Administrator access. If you're the only person using this system, you already have it and this just works. If you bring on a practitioner later who isn't an Administrator, you'll need to set their hours up for them the first time.

Add your services

  1. Open Paramedical Tattoo › Configuration › Services.

  2. Add one row per thing you offer — a consultation, a patch test, a treatment session, a touch-up. Set the Duration and a Buffer After (cleanup and prep time before the next appointment can start).

  3. Tick Bookable Online for anything a patient should be able to request themselves — typically just the consultation. Leave it off for anything that needs your eyes on the patient first.

  4. For an actual ink session, tick Requires a Clear Patch Test and/or Requires Signed Treatment Consent, and Creates a Treatment Session (see Documenting a session). Leave all three off for a plain consultation.

Why the gates matter

A gated service can't be scheduled — by you or online — against a patient's condition until that condition has a clear patch test and/or a signed consent on file. It's not extra paperwork for its own sake; it's the system refusing to let a real session get booked ahead of the safety step.

Set your preferences

  1. Open Settings › Paramedical Appointments.

  2. Set the reminder lead time (how many days before an appointment a patient gets an email) and the online booking window (how many days ahead the booking page shows open times).

Part 2

Bringing on a patient

Someone becomes a patient in the system the moment you flag them as one — whether they booked online themselves or you're entering them after an in-person consultation.

  1. Open Paramedical Tattoo › Patients › New, or find them under Contacts and tick Is a Patient near the top of the form.

  2. A Patient # is assigned automatically the moment you do — you'll never need to make one up or track the sequence yourself.

  3. Open the new Patient tab and fill in date of birth, emergency contact, and referring provider if there is one.

  4. Under Safety History, record anything that matters before a needle touches skin — bleeding disorders, blood thinners, keloid history, known allergies — and anything else worth a note.

Someone booked online

A patient who requests an appointment through the website is flagged and numbered automatically — there's nothing to set up on your end before their first visit. Fill in their safety history at the consultation.

Part 3

The condition record

Everything that follows — consent, patch tests, sessions — hangs off a condition. Open one per issue you're treating.

  1. From the patient's Conditions button, or Paramedical Tattoo › Conditions › New.

  2. Choose the type — areola restoration, scar camouflage, scalp micropigmentation, vitiligo repigmentation, or other with your own description — and the area.

  3. Set yourself as the Primary Practitioner. Once you have a second practitioner, this is what keeps one patient's care with one person across every visit.

  4. Move the status along the top from Intake to In Treatment once you've started, and to Completed or Discontinued when it's done.

The condition's three tabs — Consents, Patch Tests, Sessions — are where the rest of this handbook's work actually happens.

Part 4

Informed consent

One record per consent — treatment, patch test, or photo release — each with its own printable form.

  1. On the condition, open the Consents tab and add a row for the type you need.

  2. Open it and use the Print menu (top right) to get the form — its wording changes automatically depending on the type. Print it, have the patient sign, and keep the paper copy.

  3. Click Mark Signed to record that it's done. This is what a gated service checks for — a printed-but-unsigned consent doesn't count.

Photo release is separate

Whether you may use a patient's photos in your portfolio is its own consent, distinct from the treatment consent that lets you perform the procedure. A patient can say yes to one and no to the other.

Part 5

Patch testing

A documented allergy check before the pigment you're about to use goes anywhere near a real session.

  1. On the condition, open the Patch Tests tab and add a row: the pigment name, its lot number, and today's date.

  2. The read date defaults to three days later — adjust it if you're reading it sooner or later.

  3. When you check it, set the result to Clear or Reaction (with notes). This is what a gated service looks at — only the most recent patch test on a condition counts, so a fresh clear test always supersedes an old one.

Part 6

Scheduling appointments

Book it yourself, or confirm one a patient requested online — both land in the same calendar.

Booking one yourself

  1. Open Paramedical Tattoo › Appointments › New.

  2. Pick the patient, the service, and — for anything gated — the condition it's for. Set the start time; the system works out the end time from the service's duration and buffer, and won't let you double-book yourself.

Confirming a request from the website

  1. Open Paramedical Tattoo › Clinical › Needs Confirmation.

  2. Review it and click Confirm. The patient gets a confirmation email automatically.

On the day, and after

  • Didn't show up? Click No-show.
  • Plans changed? Click Cancel.
  • Was it a real treatment session (the service was flagged Creates a Treatment Session)? Click Complete — this creates the linked treatment-session record for you, pre-filled with the practitioner, date, and area, ready for you to fill in the clinical detail. See the next part.

Part 7

Documenting a session

Whether it was created automatically by completing an appointment, or you open one directly under the condition's Sessions tab.

  1. Fill in the pigment name and lot number, the area treated, and any numbing agent used.

  2. Write your clinical notes, and tick whether aftercare instructions were given (with notes on what you told them).

  3. Upload photos under the Before Photos and After Photos tabs. Sessions on a condition are numbered automatically in date order, so its healing progression stays easy to follow.

  4. If anything went wrong, tick Adverse Reaction and describe it — and set a Next Session Recommended Date if there's a follow-up planned.

Part 8

The patient's online booking

What a patient sees when they go to book or check their appointments — worth knowing so you can explain it if they ask.

  1. They sign in and go to My Appointments, or straight to /my/book.

  2. They only ever see services you've marked Bookable Online. A gated service — one requiring a patch test or consent — only shows up once they already have a condition on file that satisfies it. A brand-new patient will only ever see a consultation, never a treatment session, until you've assessed them in person and opened a condition.

  3. Picking a service shows real open times for the next few weeks; picking one sends a request that lands in your Needs Confirmation list.

  4. They can cancel their own upcoming appointment from My Appointments, but only more than 24 hours out — closer than that, they need to call you.

Nothing clinical — no condition, consent, patch test, or session detail — is ever visible to a patient online. That side of the system stays entirely yours.

Part 9

The routine rhythm

A rough cadence once you're up and running.

WhenDo this
Each dayCheck Needs Confirmation for overnight booking requests.
After each visitComplete the appointment; fill in the treatment session's clinical detail and photos while it's fresh.
OngoingRead patch tests on their due date and record the result before the linked session gets booked.
WeeklyGlance at the appointments calendar for the week ahead; confirm anything still pending.
As you growAdding a second practitioner? Give them a login, add a Practitioner record with their own working hours, and set which services they're qualified for on each service's Qualified Practitioners list — leave it empty to allow anyone.

Reference

Glossary

Condition
What's being treated — the record every consent, patch test, and session for that issue is attached to.
Patch test
A small application of the actual pigment, read a few days later to check for a reaction before it's used in a real session.
Gated service
A service that can't be booked against a condition until a patch test is clear and/or a treatment consent is signed for it.
Buffer time
Cleanup and prep time blocked on the calendar right after an appointment, before the next one can start.
Treatment session
One clinical visit's record — pigment, area, notes, aftercare, photos — numbered in order within its condition.
Requested / Confirmed
An appointment's first two states — requested means a patient asked for it online and it's waiting on you.
Working Hours
A practitioner's schedule — the days and times the booking page treats as open, time off included.
Adverse reaction
Any unwanted response to pigment or procedure, flagged on a patch test or a session so it's part of the permanent record.

Keep this open in a tab. When something on screen doesn't match what's written here, trust the screen — the menus move as the software updates.
